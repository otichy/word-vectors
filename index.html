03 From co-occurrence to similarity

Shared evidence, similar pressures

“Cat” and “dog” never occur together in this toy corpus. But both occur with “sleeps” and “eats”. Learning to predict the same contexts gives their target vectors similar pressures. Change “dog” to occur mainly with “moves” and “stops” and compare it with the vehicles.

What the model actually learns

This is a small skip-gram with negative sampling model. It learns two sets of vectors: targets and contexts. An observed pair encourages a higher dot product between its target and context vectors. Three randomly sampled negative pairs encourage lower dot products. Similar target vectors can emerge indirectly from shared contexts.

What the picture means

These are the model’s actual two-dimensional target vectors, not a projection of a larger space. The coordinates have no predetermined meanings such as “animal” or “vehicle”. The origin is marked; vector direction matters for cosine similarity. Emoji are labels only.

Model details and limitations

Training uses 120 passes, two learned dimensions, three negative samples per positive pair, learning rate 0.035, and a deterministic seed. Negative contexts are sampled in proportion to context frequency raised to the power 0.75, with replacement; an observed context may also be sampled as a negative. There is no subsampling or automatic context window extraction.

This toy model uses separate target and context vocabularies, supplied directly as counts. Real word2vec models typically learn from large corpora in many more dimensions. Results here are illustrative, sensitive to the evidence and training setup, and are not pretrained language knowledge. Opposites may share contexts; a static word vector also mixes different senses of the same spelling. Words with zero counts remain untrained.

Each edit retrains from deterministic initial vectors. All training histories shown are for the current table. The chart uses equal distance per coordinate unit on both axes, with its scale fixed across the current training history. Individual word initialisations depend on their spelling and role; renaming a word changes its initialisation.
